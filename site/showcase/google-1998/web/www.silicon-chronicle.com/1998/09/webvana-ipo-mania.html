Silicon Valley IPO Fever: Why Revenue Takes a Back Seat to "Eyeballs"

MENLO PARK, Calif. -- Sept. 14, 1998 -- At Sand Hill Road breakfast tables this autumn, price-to-earnings ratios have vanished from pitch decks, replaced by "price-per-registered-eyeball" and quarterly marketing burn.

Look no further than the undisputed dot-com bubble poster child or hosiery mergers like SockDrop.com Acquires ToeWarmerNet in All-Stock Swap: startups spend three dollars on Super Bowl teasers and free freight to collect one dollar of gross sales, trusting public investors will bid shares skyward on first-day NASDAQ trading.

Utility analysts caution that even boom-era traders depend on power and settlement infrastructure -- see NERC Regional Power Grid Y2K Compliance Progress Report (Q3 1998).
