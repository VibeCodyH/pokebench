The Famous $year + 1900 Bug: Fixing 19100 Dates in Perl CGI
PerlMonk 5.004 Archive | Randall Mercer & Contributors | 09-Sep-1998

In Perl, (localtime)[5] returns years elapsed since 1900: in 1998 it is 98, in 1999 it is 99, and on January 1, 2000 it becomes 100. Scripts that interpolate "19$year" suddenly display the infamous year 19100!

# BUGGY Y2K CODE:
my $year = (localtime)[5];
print "Copyright 19$year";   # prints 19100 in 2000!

# Y2K FIXED CODE:
my $full_year = (localtime)[5] + 1900;
print "Copyright $full_year";  # prints 2000

Production Note: Audit every guestbook, copyright footer, and cookie expiry generator before Dec 1999.

Perl 5.004 Environment & Testing Note: Verify scripts from the shell with perl -wcT script.cgi before copying them into /usr/local/apache/cgi-bin. Under Apache mod_perl or standard CGI, inspect /var/log/httpd/error_log with tail -f to catch premature end-of-script-headers and uninitialized $_ warnings.
